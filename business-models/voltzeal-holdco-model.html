02 — Money flow for one new contract

Deployment

1
Customer signs ESCO contract. New project SPV incorporated.
2
Debt raised at SPV level — 65–75% of capex, from an NBFC/bank, secured against the customer receivable + the container itself. Non-recourse to VoltZeal HoldCo and to NK² Squared.
3
Equity raised at SPV level — Platform HoldCo funds ~80%, VoltZeal HoldCo contributes a small promote stake (~20%) for IP + O&M, not cash.
4
SPV buys the container from the OEM (Adani/Waaree/import) and licenses the EMS/BMS from VoltZeal HoldCo.

Ongoing cash — who gets paid, in what order

1. Lender — interest + principal on project debt, paid first, every period, from customer's savings-share collections.
2. VoltZeal HoldCo — license + O&M fee — fixed, small, covers cost of running the system. Paid whether or not the site has hit its savings target yet.
3. Platform HoldCo — capital return + preferred hurdle — NK²'s equity check comes back first, plus an agreed hurdle rate, before anyone shares in upside.
4. Residual split — above the hurdle, Platform HoldCo and VoltZeal HoldCo split remaining profit per their SPV equity stake (~80:20).

VoltZeal HoldCo's own income = license/O&M fees (all SPVs) + promote distributions (performing SPVs). That income, not project capex, is what eventually reaches VoltZeal HoldCo's own shareholders — NK² Squared and the founders — as dividends.

03 — Why 26%+ in VoltZeal is necessary and fair

This section is written to be shown directly to the VoltZeal founders.

  • Capital risk needs matching governance. NK² carries 100% of the capex and credit risk on every site — ₹8–10cr each, dozens of sites. Board rights should track where the risk sits, same as any funding round.
  • 26% is a floor, not a power grab. It's the minimum needed for statutory blocking rights under company law — a veto on things that could put invested capital at risk (IP sale, winding up, related-party deals), not day-to-day control.
  • Future equity value comes from the platform, not the product alone. VoltZeal's value from here scales with NK²'s capital and customer access, not standalone software sales. A shared cap table reflects where that growth actually comes from.
  • Founders keep operating control. CEO/CTO roles, product roadmap, majority board seats at the 51% tier — this is a capital and governance split, not a management takeover.
  • Founders' realized value can be higher, not lower. Reverse vesting + earn-outs tied to real performance + a fresh ESOP pool funded from NK²'s stake — the platform scaling faster with committed capital can outrun what VoltZeal earns staying independent.
  • This is standard PE/VC practice. Whoever funds balance-sheet risk gets proportionate governance rights. Nothing about this deal is unusual.
  • The path to 80% is phased, not immediate. Start at 51%, move to 80% via a pre-agreed call option over 3–4 years — founders see the model work before ceding more.

04 — If VoltZeal doesn't agree to become the HoldCo

Fallback: keep VoltZeal fully independent, but lock in exclusivity and defensibility contractually.

  1. Take a minority stake instead — 20–26% of VoltZeal, with a board seat and information rights. No HoldCo conversion.
  2. Build NK² Holdco separately — the original structure: NK² Holdco owns the project SPVs directly; VoltZeal stays outside it.
  3. Sign a long-term exclusive supply & license agreement — VoltZeal sells its EMS/BMS bundle only through NK² in India, for 5–10 years. Make it mutual: NK² commits to minimum deployment volumes in return.
  4. Pay for the exclusivity — upfront fee or guaranteed minimum royalty. Makes it a purchased right, not an unpaid restraint.
  5. Escrow the source code — released to NK² on breach, insolvency, or persistent underperformance.
  6. Add personal non-compete/non-solicit on founders — enforceable for the contract term under Indian law (not beyond it — plan around this limit, don't rely on it alone).
  7. Keep a ROFR and a call option — right to buy more of VoltZeal later, at a pre-agreed formula, if trust builds or VoltZeal's position changes.
  8. Build independent moats in parallel — own the customer contracts, own the fleet performance data, and qualify a second EMS/BMS vendor over the exclusivity term so NK² isn't permanently single-vendor dependent.
